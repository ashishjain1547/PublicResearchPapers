🧑‍⚖️ LawBot: Making Legal Help Simple — critique & analysis

Paper33458  |  Sahil Ahire, Riya Jain, Karan Borse, Prof Anuja Gaikwad

🏛️ Architecture

Modular RAG + LLM pipeline – the system uses a modern retrieval‑augmented generation architecture with clear stages:

  • Frontend UI – chat interface with dark mode, chat history sidebar, document upload, and multilingual toggle.
  • Backend (Flask/FastAPI) – handles requests, orchestrates retrieval and LLM calls.
  • Knowledge base – static legal Q&A / chunks (JSON/CSV) + dynamic user‑uploaded documents (PDF, DOCX, TXT).
  • Retrieval – chunking (200–500 words) + embeddings + similarity search (vector search).
  • LLM (Mistral) – generates responses using retrieved context (RAG).
  • Multilingual layer – language detection → translation → process → translate back.
  • Storage – SQLite / MongoDB for chat history.

✅ Architecture is well‑designed and follows industry best practices (RAG, vector search, chunking, history). The flowchart (Fig 1) and component descriptions are thorough.

🕰️ How outdated is the used / referred technology stack?

📚 Stack components

  • Mistral (LLM) – state‑of‑the‑art open‑weight model (2023–2024).
  • RAG + embeddings + vector search – cutting‑edge for knowledge‑intensive tasks.
  • Flask/FastAPI – modern Python backends.
  • PyMuPDF / pdfplumber / Tesseract OCR – standard document parsers.
  • Translation API – modern multilingual support.
  • Dark mode, chat history sidebar – contemporary UX.

⏳ Currency assessment

The technology stack is very current (2024–2026). The use of Mistral, RAG, vector search, and multilingual translation is exactly what modern legal chatbots use. The paper also references transformer‑based models (BERT, GPT, XLM‑R) and retrieval‑augmented generation – all state‑of‑the‑art.

📈 No outdated tech – the stack is production‑ready and well‑chosen.

🧪 How was the solution tested?

Comprehensive evaluation – the paper provides both quantitative and qualitative metrics:

  • Quantitative (Table 2): Response time (p95 latency: 2.4s simple, 4.8s complex), Usefulness Score (~85%), Source Accuracy/Groundedness (95.5%), nDCG@5 (0.89), Recall@10 (0.93), scalability (50 concurrent users).
  • Qualitative: Tested with legal queries (cybercrime, property, consumer, divorce), document uploads, multilingual (Hindi), and chat history.
  • Error analysis: Identified issues with broad queries, scanned documents, translation of legal terms, and very specific scenarios.

✅ This is the most rigorously evaluated paper among the three – with clear metrics and honest limitations.

📂 What data sets were used?

The paper provides detailed dataset statistics:

  • Static Legal Knowledge: 5–8 categories (Cybercrime, Family, Property, Consumer, Criminal), ~50–150 topics, ~200–500 Q&A pairs or ~300–800 chunks, 80–250 words per entry.
  • User‑Uploaded Documents: PDF, DOCX, TXT (5–10 MB), 1–20 pages, chunk size 200–500 words, OCR for scanned files.
  • Sources: Government sites, legal FAQs, acts/sections, verified legal blogs.
  • Format: JSON/CSV/DB, with metadata (category, keywords, source).

📌 While the dataset is handcrafted and not a public benchmark, the paper is transparent about its size, structure, and cleaning process.

✅ Plus points of the research

💡 Technical & UX strengths

  • Modern RAG + Mistral – uses state‑of‑the‑art LLM with retrieval for grounded responses.
  • Multilingual support – language detection + translation makes it accessible across India.
  • Document upload – users can upload legal files and get document‑based answers.
  • Chat history – persistent conversations with sidebar (like ChatGPT).
  • Dark mode – modern, user‑friendly UI.
  • Thorough evaluation – latency, accuracy, retrieval metrics, scalability, and error analysis.

🔍 Practical vision

  • Clear ethical disclaimer – not a replacement for lawyers, just a first step.
  • Addresses real‑world problems – language barriers, cost, complexity of legal texts.
  • Comprehensive literature survey – from ELIZA to GPT‑4, RAG, and legal NLP.
  • Detailed future roadmap – voice I/O, login, encryption, escalation, mobile app, analytics.
  • Well‑structured dataset preparation – sprints, cleaning, structuring, validation.

❌ Gaps in the research

  • Dataset not public – the legal knowledge base is hand‑curated and not shared, so reproducibility is limited.
  • No comparison with baselines – no evaluation against keyword‑based, rule‑based, or other LLM‑based systems.
  • Translation quality – the paper admits minor translation issues for legal terms; no rigorous evaluation of translation accuracy.
  • Scalability tested only up to 50 users – not clear if it handles hundreds or thousands of concurrent users.
  • Security / privacy – mentioned as future work; uploaded documents may contain sensitive info.
  • Dependence on external translation API – may introduce cost and latency.
  • No fine‑tuning – uses a generic Mistral model; fine‑tuning on legal corpora could improve accuracy.

🧠 Ideas to learn and use in “Your Legal” solution

  • Adopt RAG with Mistral or similar – use retrieval‑augmented generation for grounded, hallucination‑reduced answers.
  • Implement chat history + sidebar – allows users to resume conversations, improving usability.
  • Document upload with chunking + vector search – enables document‑based Q&A for agreements, notices, etc.
  • Multilingual support – language detection + translation layer to serve users in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, etc.
  • Dark mode and modern UI – makes the tool feel professional and comfortable.
  • Rigorous evaluation – measure latency, groundedness, nDCG, recall, and user satisfaction – as done in this paper.
  • Error analysis – document common failure modes (broad queries, scanned docs, translation issues) and plan fixes.
  • Future features – voice I/O, login, encryption, lawyer escalation, mobile app – all valuable for “Your Legal”.

🚀 Your Legal can adopt the entire blueprint from this paper – RAG + Mistral + multilingual + document upload – while addressing the gaps: use a public legal corpus (e.g., Indian Kanoon), implement fine‑tuning on legal data, and add strong privacy/security from day one.

📌 Final verdict: This is the strongest paper among the three reviewed. It presents a modern, well‑architected, and empirically evaluated legal chatbot. The use of RAG, Mistral, multilingual support, document upload, and chat history is state‑of‑the‑art. The evaluation is thorough (latency, groundedness, retrieval metrics, qualitative analysis). While the dataset is not public and some minor gaps exist (translation quality, baseline comparison), this paper serves as an excellent reference for building a production‑ready legal assistant. For “Your Legal”, follow this blueprint closely and enhance it with fine‑tuning, public legal corpora, and stronger privacy measures.
