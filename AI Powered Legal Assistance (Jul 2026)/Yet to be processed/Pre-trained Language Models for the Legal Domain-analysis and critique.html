⚖️ Pre-trained Language Models for the Legal Domain — critique & analysis

arXiv:2209.06049v5  |  Paul, Mandal, Goyal, Ghosh (IIT Kharagpur)

🏛️ Architecture

Transformer‑based PLM with HierBERT fine‑tuning – the paper presents a rigorous study of pre‑trained language models for Indian legal text. The core architecture involves:

  • Pre‑training: BERT‑base (12 layers, 768 hidden, 12 heads) with MLM + NSP objectives. Three variants: InLegalBERT (continued pre‑training of LegalBERT), InCaseLawBERT (continued pre‑training of CaseLawBERT), and CustomInLawBERT (trained from scratch with Indian‑specific vocabulary).
  • Fine‑tuning: HierBERT architecture (lower‑level BERT encoder + upper BiLSTM with attention) for long documents, with task‑specific heads (linear/CRF).
  • Data pipeline: Chunking into 254‑token segments, dynamic masking, whole‑word masking, and dynamic NSP sampling.

✅ Architecture is state‑of‑the‑art for legal NLP – BERT‑based, hierarchical processing, and carefully designed pre‑training. The HierBERT design is well‑suited for long legal documents.

🕰️ How outdated is the used / referred technology stack?

📚 Stack components

  • BERT‑base – 2018, but still a solid foundation; RoBERTa/Longformer are more recent.
  • LegalBERT / CaseLawBERT – 2020–2021, still relevant.
  • HierBERT + BiLSTM + Attention – modern for long‑document tasks.
  • PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, Datasets – current and actively maintained.
  • WordPiece tokenizer with custom Indian vocabulary – tailored approach.

⏳ Currency assessment

The technology stack is modern (2020–2022) and well‑chosen for the task. The paper uses BERT‑base rather than more recent LLMs (e.g., GPT‑3/4, Mistral), but this is intentional for controlled comparison and reproducibility. The HierBERT architecture is still used in legal NLP today. The paper does not use retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) or generative LLMs, but that is outside its scope (it focuses on discriminative tasks).

📈 The stack is not outdated – it represents best practices for PLM‑based legal NLP as of 2022.

🧪 How was the solution tested?

Rigorous and comprehensive evaluation – the paper is a benchmark‑style study with:

  • Pre‑training quality: Perplexity on a held‑out test set (10% of Indian legal corpus).
  • Three downstream tasks across five datasets (Indian and non‑Indian): Legal Statute Identification (ILSI, ECtHR‑B), Semantic Segmentation (ISS, UKSS), and Court Judgment Prediction (ILDC‑multi).
  • Metrics: Macro‑precision, macro‑recall, macro‑F1 (5‑fold cross‑validation).
  • Statistical significance: Wilcoxon Signed‑Rank Test.
  • Explainability: KL‑divergence between model attention and expert annotations (ILDC‑expert).
  • Comparison with prior SOTA: Outperforms original works on 4 out of 5 datasets.

✅ This is the most rigorously evaluated paper among all reviewed – with quantitative metrics, statistical tests, and qualitative explainability analysis.

📂 What data sets were used?

Large‑scale Indian legal corpus for pre‑training:

  • ~5.4 million documents, ~27 GB raw text – Supreme Court, 26 High Courts, 2 District Courts, and 1,113 Central Government Acts (32,021 Sections).
  • Time period: 1950–2019 (with some 1800s cases).
  • Pre‑processing: Noise removal (artefacts from digitisation), chunking into 254‑token segments.

Downstream datasets:

  • ILSI (Indian Statute Identification) – from Paul et al. (2022).
  • ECtHR‑B (European Court of Human Rights) – from Chalkidis et al. (2022).
  • ISS (Indian Semantic Segmentation) – from Bhattacharya et al. (2019).
  • UKSS (UK Semantic Segmentation) – from Bhattacharya et al. (2021).
  • ILDC‑multi (Court Judgment Prediction) – from Malik et al. (2021).
  • ILDC‑expert – 56 documents with expert annotations for explainability.

📌 All datasets are publicly available benchmarks – excellent for reproducibility.

✅ Plus points of the research

💡 Technical contributions

  • First large‑scale PLM study for Indian law – fills a critical gap.
  • Three PLM variants – re‑trained LegalBERT, re‑trained CaseLawBERT, and from‑scratch with custom vocabulary.
  • Comprehensive evaluation – three tasks, five datasets, both Indian and non‑Indian.
  • Explainability analysis – compares model attention with expert annotations using KL‑divergence.
  • Public release – models and code on HuggingFace and GitHub.
  • State‑of‑the‑art results – outperforms prior works on 4 out of 5 datasets.

🔍 Practical insights

  • Re‑training vs. from‑scratch – shows that re‑training is more cost‑effective with limited compute.
  • Adaptability of LegalBERT – LegalBERT is more adaptable to new domains than CaseLawBERT.
  • Custom vocabulary helps – but not enough to beat re‑trained models with limited steps.
  • Indian legal text is noisy – pre‑processing and domain‑specific pre‑training are crucial.

❌ Gaps in the research

  • Limited pre‑training steps – due to compute constraints, models were trained for fewer steps than prior works (300k–700k vs. 1M–2M).
  • No generative / conversational tasks – focuses only on discriminative tasks (classification, segmentation). No Q&A or chatbot evaluation.
  • No RAG or LLM integration – does not explore retrieval‑augmented generation or large generative models.
  • No multilingual evaluation – all tasks are in English (Indian legal text is in English).
  • Explainability only for CJPE – KL‑divergence analysis is limited to one task and a small expert dataset.
  • No bias or fairness analysis – legal AI must be fair; not addressed.
  • Pre‑training data skew – temporal skew (recent cases dominate) may introduce bias.

🧠 Ideas to learn and use in “Your Legal” solution

  • Use InLegalBERT as a base encoder – it is publicly available and outperforms general BERT on Indian legal tasks.
  • Adopt HierBERT for long documents – chunk‑then‑encode with BiLSTM + attention is effective for legal texts.
  • Build a custom vocabulary – Indian legal text has unique terms; a tailored tokenizer improves performance.
  • Continue pre‑training (re‑training) rather than from‑scratch – more efficient with limited compute.
  • Include explainability – use attention scores or LIME/SHAP to show which parts of the document influenced the answer.
  • Leverage public Indian legal datasets – ILDC, ILSI, ISS are available for fine‑tuning and evaluation.
  • Extend to generative tasks – use InLegalBERT as a backbone for a RAG‑based conversational legal assistant.
  • Address bias and fairness – analyse model predictions across different demographics and case types.

🚀 Your Legal can adopt InLegalBERT + HierBERT as the core NLP engine, combine it with RAG + generative LLM for conversational Q&A, and leverage the public Indian legal datasets for fine‑tuning and evaluation.

📌 Final verdict: This is the most rigorous and impactful paper among all reviewed. It provides a benchmark‑quality study on PLMs for Indian law, with clear methodology, comprehensive evaluation, and public release of models and code. The findings (re‑training LegalBERT is effective, custom vocabulary helps, HierBERT works well) are directly applicable to “Your Legal”. The main gaps are the lack of generative / conversational tasks and RAG, but these can be addressed by extending the work. Strongly recommended as a foundation for any Indian legal NLP project.
